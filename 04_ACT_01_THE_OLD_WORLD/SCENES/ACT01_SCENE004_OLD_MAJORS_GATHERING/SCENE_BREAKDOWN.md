### Lessons for every future count-critical shot

1. Never write vague phrases such as "a few animals", "other animals" or "background silhouettes" when the count matters. Name exact counts and attach the exact approved group references.
2. A text instruction saying "four sheep" is not proof that four will remain visible. V02 changed from three readable sheep to two later in the clip. Verify count across the full duration, not just the first frame.
3. For this shot, use three fixed zones: **LEFT = 2 cows; CENTRE = Boxer + Clover + Benjamin; RIGHT = 4 sheep**.
4. The four sheep must stand in one straight horizontal row, at the same camera depth, with a visible gap between each. Each must have a separately visible head, neck, torso and legs; all four full bodies must remain in frame. No stagger, overlap, blocking, post/trough/shadow occlusion or edge crop.
5. All nine animals start in position at frame 0 and stay there. Fixed tripod only. No walking, crossing, entering, exiting, dolly, pan, tilt, zoom, cut or reframing. Only tiny breathing, blinking and ear motion.
6. Before spending video credits on another strict-count shot, use an owner-approved still composition check if possible. Count all nine in that still first. If still-image generation has a cost, disclose the live price and obtain separate owner approval before spending. Do not assume image generation is free.
7. At review, inspect 0s, 2s, 4s, 6s, 8s and near the final frame. Count all nine at each point. Missing, merged, hidden, duplicated or changing animals = continuity failure; do not approve.
